My paper, "Immunotherapy: PD1, Immune Evasion, and the Importance of Pembrolizumab", explains in depth how genetic mutations can cause abnormal cell growth that leads to diseases such as cancer. These mutations can result from biological and environmental factors. Immunotherapy is when the body uses its own immune system to target and eliminate cancer cells. The three major types of immunotherapy are immunostimulants, T-cell therapy, and monoclonal antibodies. My paper focuses on checkpoint inhibitors (a type of monoclonal antibody), which help T-cells remain active. The PD-1 pathway (Programmed Cell Death Protein 1) usually prevents the body’s immune system from engaging in excessive immune responses. Cancer cells can use this pathway through the ligand PD-L1, disrupting T-cell activity and enabling immune evasion. Checkpoint inhibitors block the binding between PD-1 and its ligand, PD-L1, allowing T-cells to remain active. Pembrolizumab, also known as Keytruda, blocks PD-1 and is effective in treating non-small cell lung cancer. Overall, my paper highlights the potential benefits of immunotherapy in modern cancer treatment.
