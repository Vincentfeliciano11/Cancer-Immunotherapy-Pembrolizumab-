What you are looking at

Pembrolizumab, known as Keytruda, is a monoclonal antibody and checkpoint inhibitor. Pembrolizumab binds to the PD-1 receptor on T-cells, blocking the interaction of PD-1 with PD-L1 and PD-L2. By reducing this interaction, pembrolizumab allows T-cells to stay active, improving their ability to identify and eliminate cancer cells.

This molecular view helps show the physical interaction behind that explanation: the variable regions of pembrolizumab contact the extracellular portion of PD-1.

Scientific accuracy note: PDB 5B8C is not a photograph from a clinical trial and it is not the entire commercial antibody. It is an experimentally determined 2.15 Å X-ray crystal structure of pembrolizumab's Fv (variable heavy/light) fragment bound to the extracellular domain of human PD-1. Experimental structural data are the appropriate source for a molecular-level 3D configuration.
